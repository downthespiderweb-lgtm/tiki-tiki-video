## 1. Concept statement

- **Name:** Tiki Tiki Lounge & Bar
- **Location:** Pacific Beach, San Diego, California
- **Concept:** a tropical-inspired restaurant and lounge combining coastal and Baja-influenced food,
  creative tropical cocktails, a relaxed social atmosphere and live entertainment.
- **Physical environment:** large central island-style tiki bar · warm wood finishes and tropical
  decorations · lush plants and greenery · comfortable booth seating · small stage for live music ·
  outdoor patio · open, inviting layout suited to socializing.
- **What makes it distinctive:** the whole social evening (Baja-coastal food, a signature tropical
  cocktail program, live music and an indoor/outdoor setting) in one place, rather than a restaurant
  *or* a bar *or* a music venue.
- **Why Pacific Beach (concept-based argument, not market research):** we believe Pacific Beach's
  beach lifestyle, social nightlife energy and mix of locals and visitors suit a fun, tropical,
  social concept. No market data was gathered to verify this; it should be validated (foot traffic,
  competition, lease availability, local licensing conditions) before any commitment.

## 2. Guests (target-market assumptions — not verified statistics)

- **Primary target:** adults ages 21–35.
- **Segments:** college students and young adults · young professionals · couples on dates ·
  groups of friends · local San Diego residents · tourists and visitors.
- **Demographic considerations:** students and working professionals; individuals, couples and
  groups; local residents and visitors.
- **Psychographics:** enjoy socializing with friends; appreciate attractive tropical environments;
  seek memorable dining and nightlife experiences; enjoy creative cocktails and distinctive food;
  value live music, relaxed entertainment and outdoor seating; like discovering visually appealing
  places to visit and share; want a place that works for casual gatherings, dates and nights out.
- **Why they would choose us / occasions:** casual after-work or after-class gatherings, date
  nights and nights out. The atmosphere (tiki bar, greenery, patio, live music), the shareable
  Baja-coastal menu and the signature cocktail program are designed around time spent together.
- No survey results or market-size figures are claimed.

## 5. Financials — preliminary estimates

> These are preliminary project estimates — **not** verified financial statements, supplier quotes
> or proof of profitability. Values marked *derived* were calculated from the estimates; nothing was
> adjusted to make the concept look more profitable.

| Item | Estimate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Restaurant size | 3,000 sq ft | — |
| **Annual sales target** | **$1,500,000** | — |
| of which beverage sales (assumption) | $450,000 | part of the $1.5M, **not** extra revenue |
| of which food sales (*derived*) | $1,050,000 | $1,500,000 − $450,000 |
| Labor | $547,500 | 36.5% of total sales |
| Food cost | $480,000 | 32% of total sales |
| Rent | $200,000 | annual |
| Alcohol cost | $112,500 | 25% of $450,000 beverage sales |
| Utilities | $60,000 | 4% of total sales |
| Manager salary | $52,000 | annual |
| Payment processing | $45,000 | 3% of total sales |
| Insurance | $3,500 | annual |
| Licenses | $3,000 | annual |
| POS & accounting | $2,000 | annual |
| **Total listed expenses** (*derived*) | **$1,505,500** | sum of the ten lines above |
| **Preliminary remainder** (*derived*) | **−$5,500** | $1,500,000 − $1,505,500 |
| Remainder if manager salary is already inside labor (*derived*) | +$46,500 | −$5,500 + $52,000 |

**Calculation check:** 200,000 + 3,500 + 52,000 + 3,000 + 2,000 + 480,000 + 547,500 + 112,500 +
60,000 + 45,000 = **1,505,500**. All brief percentages verify (e.g. 0.365 × 1,500,000 = 547,500).

**Other derived indicators (illustrative, from the estimates):**
- Prime cost (food + alcohol + labor) = $1,140,000 = 76% of sales (79.5% if the manager salary is
  additional to labor).
- Food cost as a share of *food* sales = $480,000 ÷ $1,050,000 = **45.7%** — the 32% figure is
  measured against total sales, so food is costlier relative to food revenue than it first appears.
- Rent = 13.3% of sales ≈ $66.67 per sq ft per year (fixed regardless of sales).
- Sales per sq ft = $500. Daily sales needed ≈ $4,110 *if open 365 days* (operating days not yet set).
- Each 1% of sales = $15,000.

**Possible double-counting:** if the $52,000 manager salary is already included in the $547,500
labor estimate, listing it separately double-counts it. Removing it changes the remainder from
−$5,500 to +$46,500 (about 3.1% of sales). This must be confirmed.

**The remainder is not verified net profit.** Costs not yet established include: repairs and
maintenance · marketing and promotion · operating supplies (smallwares, paper goods, cleaning) ·
food and beverage waste/spoilage · taxes (income, property, payroll taxes and benefits if not in
labor) · live-entertainment fees · linen/laundry, waste removal, pest control, music licensing,
equipment leases or loan payments, opening and pre-opening costs, depreciation.

**Risks:**
- *Sales target:* $1.5M is an assumption; a shortfall hits a business with a large fixed rent.
- *Labor:* 36.5% of sales is high and sensitive to scheduling, wages and turnover.
- *Food cost:* 45.7% of food sales leaves little margin on food; waste or price increases worsen it.
- *Beverage sales:* the $450,000 (30% of sales) mix drives margin; if guests buy fewer drinks,
  revenue and margin fall together.
- *Occupancy:* $200,000 rent (13.3% of sales) is fixed and does not fall if sales disappoint.

**What must be validated before deciding viability:** realistic sales (covers, average check,
operating days, seasonality), the beverage mix, actual supplier and menu costing, labor schedules
and wage rates (and whether the manager is inside labor), the actual lease terms, insurance and
license quotes, and every missing cost above — ideally in a monthly pro forma with a break-even
analysis and a sensitivity range.

**Honest conclusion:** the concept has clear guest appeal and a coherent menu, but the listed
estimates currently show a small shortfall (or, at best, a thin ~3% remainder before many missing
costs). It is not yet proven viable. It would need tighter labor scheduling, menu engineering to
bring food cost down relative to food sales, a strong beverage program, and a favorable lease —
and the numbers must be validated before investing.

- **Hiring process:** clear job descriptions → structured interviews → practical skill assessments
  where appropriate (e.g. kitchen tryouts, bartending tests) → reference checks → verification of
  required qualifications (food handler, responsible alcohol service, work eligibility).
- **Training:** menu knowledge · food safety and sanitation · customer service · teamwork and
  communication · workplace safety and emergency procedures · responsible alcohol service and
  applicable requirements.

- **Why it matters:** skilled, well-scheduled and well-trained staff deliver consistent service
  and food quality, which drives guest satisfaction and repeat visits; lower turnover reduces
  hiring and training costs and protects profitability.
